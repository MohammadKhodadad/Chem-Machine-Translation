\section{Results and Analysis}
\label{sec:results}

We analyze ten current model runs on Chemical Patents, JRC Articles, and JRC Definitions under
zero-shot and few-shot prompting, using 84 checksum-verified aggregate summaries. Historical runs
and the small native sample are excluded. Each metric averages directions shared across all
models and both prompts within a dataset; shared directions do not establish identical scored
segments. The findings below are descriptive, without confidence intervals or significance tests.

\paragraph{Model comparison.}
Figure~\ref{fig:results-ranks} summarizes Chosen~01 using the Luna terminology view. The mixed
ranking weights semantic, lexical, and terminology family mean ranks equally, then averages the
six dataset/prompt strata equally. Terminology gives equal weight to all, externally verified,
and LLM-enriched groups. GPT-6 Luna ranks first, followed by Muse Glimmer and Gemma. Muse has a
better lexical family rank than Gemma, whereas Gemma has a better semantic family rank,
illustrating the information lost in a single aggregate ordering. These ranks depend on the
model roster and weighting rule; they are not estimates of an absolute quality difference.

\paragraph{Prompting and terminology.}
Figure~\ref{fig:results-prompting} condenses Chosen~04 to COMET and variant-aware coverage of
externally verified terms. Few-shot COMET increases for 7/10 patent models, 8/10 article models,
and 6/10 definition models. Corresponding coverage increases occur for 6/10, 10/10, and 6/10
models. Muse's COMET changes are $+0.012$, $+0.014$, and $+0.015$, respectively; Tiny Aya Fire's
definition COMET decreases by $0.023$, alongside a $6.16$ percentage-point coverage decrease.
Thus few-shot prompting does not uniformly improve either dimension. External verification
denotes upstream evidence, not expert-validated terminology correctness.

\paragraph{Scope and supplementary results.}
The supplement retains full scorecards, all-metric prompting bars, and latency and API-cost plots
(Chosen~02--03). Latency reflects different deployments; cost reprices recorded tokens rather than
measuring expenditure. These diagnostics establish neither controlled efficiency nor a causal
benefit of oracle terms. Matched baseline/oracle comparisons and independent error review remain
future work.
